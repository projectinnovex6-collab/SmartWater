About the model

Smart Water Network: Leak Detection and Automatic Isolation

Jitamitra Das, Aditya Bhusan Behera, Bignesh Acharya, Sai Swaroop Padhi · DAV Public School, CDA · Sahodaya

Undetected pipe leaks waste water and damage property, and in a small network a leak can run for hours before anyone notices. This project presents the Smart Water Network, a working model that detects a leak in a pipe section, isolates it automatically and alerts an operator.

Two YF-S201 flow sensors measure how much water enters (Q1) and how much leaves (Q2) one pipe branch. Each sensor sends pulses, about 7.5 per second for every 1 L/min. An Arduino counts the pulses on interrupt pins 2 and 3, averages the readings and computes the flow difference ΔQ = Q1 − Q2. In a healthy pipe what goes in comes out, so ΔQ stays near zero. When ΔQ stays at or above 0.40 L/min for 3 continuous seconds, the leak is confirmed; the confirmation window filters out short pump surges and prevents false alarms.

On confirmation the controller switches on a red LED and a buzzer, and a relay cuts power to a 12 V normally-closed solenoid valve so the affected section shuts off. The alarm stays latched until a person checks the pipe and resets the system. An optional ESP32 module forwards the same event to a saved operator phone number, and readings are sent to a laptop dashboard over serial. A 12 V R385 diaphragm pump drives the water. On the exhibition model the leak is created with a ball valve (the leak tap) fitted between Q1 and Q2, so the pipe is never cut.

The earlier pressure-sensor prototype cost about ₹4,850 including GST; the flow-sensor build's cost will be added once confirmed. The interactive 3D model on this page is a simulation of the physical prototype; its readings are illustrative, not measured data.

Keywords: leak detection, flow sensor, YF-S201, mass balance, Arduino, solenoid valve, ESP32, smart water

From water flow to a closed valve

  1. SenseWater spins a small rotor in each YF-S201. A Hall sensor makes pulses, about 7.5 per second for each 1 L/min.
  2. CountThe Arduino counts pulses on pins 2 and 3 with interrupts, then Q (L/min) = pulses per second ÷ 7.5.
  3. AverageAveraging a few readings smooths out noise.
  4. CompareΔQ = Q1 − Q2. A leak between the sensors lowers Q2.
  5. ConfirmΔQ at or above 0.40 L/min must hold for 3 s before it counts as a leak.
  6. ActRed LED and buzzer on, relay opens, the normally-closed valve shuts, alarm latches.
  7. ReportSerial data goes to the laptop; the optional ESP32 sends a phone alert.

Current limits

  • One branch and two sensors. A leak is found only between Q1 and Q2.
  • The 0.40 L/min threshold and 3 s window are fixed in the program.
  • A leak that lowers ΔQ by less than 0.40 L/min is not flagged.
  • The YF-S201 datasheet range is 1–30 L/min, so very small flows are less reliable until calibrated.
  • Surges shorter than 3 s are ignored on purpose.
  • This page is a simulation. Values are illustrative, not measured.

Possible next steps

  • Add more flow sensors to locate the leaking section of a longer pipe.
  • Make the threshold and delay adjustable, with a calibration routine.
  • Log readings over time to spot slow leaks.
  • Send data to a cloud dashboard through the ESP32.
